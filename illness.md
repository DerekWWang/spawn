# Illness

There are two infections that pass between people, and two other ways to fall ill. The numbers behind them are on [Survival and Physiology](Survival-and-Physiology), and the medicines on [Clothing and Medical Care](Clothing-and-Medical-Care).

| Illness | How you get it | What it does | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Respiratory infection | By chance, or from someone who has it | Mental capacity falls to 70% for two days | Antibiotic, or wait it out |
| Systemic infection | By chance (rarely), from someone who has it, or from bad water and bad food | Mental capacity falls to 50% and it does steady damage for two and a half days. It can kill | Antibiotic |
| Wound infection | A cut, a ball or a bite that festers | Slow damage until treated | Antiseptic |
| Poisoning | Untreated water, raw or spoiled food | Damage for a quarter of a day | Antitoxin |

## Falling ill by chance

Nobody has to give you a cold. Every person has a small chance each day of coming down
with one.

- Respiratory infection: 2% a day. That is about twice a year.
- Systemic infection: 0.2% a day.
- Both chances are doubled while your hunger or thirst is below 30. Starved and parched
  people get sick.

## The course of an infection

1. **Incubation.** A quarter of a day for a respiratory infection, half a day for a
   systemic one. You feel fine and nothing shows on the health screen.
2. **Infectious before you know it.** For the last quarter of the incubation you are
   already passing it on.
3. **Symptoms.** Two days (respiratory) or two and a half (systemic). The condition
   shows on the health screen and you stay infectious throughout.
4. **Immunity.** After recovery, or after an antibiotic, you cannot catch that
   infection again for 14 days.

## How it spreads

A carrier exposes the people around them. Each exposed person has a 6% chance per hour
of catching it.

| Where | Who is exposed |
| --- | --- |
| In the open | Anyone within two blocks |
| Under one roof | Anyone within six blocks in line of sight who is also under a roof |

A crowded hut shares one air. One sick person in a bunkhouse will, given a day or two,
infect most of it. The same people working outdoors a few metres apart mostly stay well.

## Keeping a settlement healthy

- Keep people fed and watered: it halves the chance of illness starting.
- Give the sick their own roof, or keep them outdoors and at a distance.
- Boil drinking water and cook meat. Most systemic infections start there.
- Treat wounds with antiseptic. Bites fester three times as often as blade cuts.
- Stock antibiotic before winter. An antibiotic taken during the incubation works too.
